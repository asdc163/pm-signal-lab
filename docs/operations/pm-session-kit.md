# PM Signal Lab — Five-Minute Session Kit

This kit is for a real PM, founder, product designer, or product engineer trying the public preview for the first time.

## Before you start

- Use the hosted preview or local URL provided by the maintainer.
- Do not paste customer names, private tickets, API keys, tokens, or confidential roadmap material.
- The sample pack is safe to use. Custom evidence stays in the current browser session and is not uploaded by this preview.
- This is a product usability session, not a claim that the product has been validated for your organisation.

## The task

1. Start from the empty first-run screen.
2. Load the sample evidence pack.
3. Open one source and answer: 「這句話真的能回到哪個來源？」
4. Go to Verify and accept, edit, or keep one claim as a hypothesis.
5. Draft the smallest experiment and read the `Known limits` / `Not covered` sections.
6. Export the decision brief and copy the session summary.

Please do the task without a walkthrough from the maintainer. Note where you hesitate, what you expect to happen, and what you would change.

## Feedback questions

- After five seconds, what did you think this product was for?
- Which step felt most useful? Which step felt unnecessary?
- Did the source mapping and limitation change how much you trusted the claim?
- Where did you hesitate or look for a control that was not there?
- Could you finish the task with keyboard only? If not, where did focus become unclear?
- On a phone, could you see the next action and press it comfortably?
- Would you bring one of your own evidence packs into this workflow? Why or why not?
- What should be fixed before you would use it with a real team?

## Session evidence format

When opening the feedback issue, include:

- `Tester role`: PM / founder / designer / engineer / other
- `Environment`: hosted URL or local URL, browser, desktop/mobile
- `Task result`: completed / partially completed / stopped
- `Hesitation`: the exact step or wording that slowed you down
- `Trust`: what made the output credible or untrustworthy
- `Recovery`: what happened after an error or backtrack
- `One change`: the single change that would make you try again
- `Permission`: confirm that the report contains no private customer data

Do not include raw customer evidence in a public issue. A maintainer may ask for a private follow-up when the report needs more context.

## What this proves

A submitted report is evidence about one person completing one task in one environment. It does not prove general usability, retention, model quality, or adoption. The maintainer should aggregate several reports before changing the product direction.
