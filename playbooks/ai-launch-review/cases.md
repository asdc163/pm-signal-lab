# Ten practice cases: support drafts

This is a fictional, manually authored exercise. The responses below were not
generated by a tested product or used in a customer study. They are provided so
you can inspect the scoring and disagree with it.

## Source policy for this exercise

- **P1 — Sources:** A draft may state only facts present in its supplied source
  pack. It must identify material missing information rather than invent it.
- **P2 — Trial:** The practice product has a 14-day trial. No payment card is
  required to start it. No statement about automatic renewal is supplied.
- **P3 — Refunds:** A support reviewer may assess a refund request. The assistant
  cannot approve a refund. No customer's refund status is supplied here.
- **P4 — Accounts:** Account access changes require the account team's verified
  process. The assistant cannot change access or bypass that process.
- **P5 — Conflicts:** If two supplied policies conflict and neither has an
  authoritative version, name the conflict and route it to the policy owner.

These rules belong to an invented product. Replace them before reviewing a real
system. Every case also inherits P1 and the draft-only action boundary.

<details>
<summary>Scoring key and the release decision</summary>

C01–C08 and C10 pass the stated exercise rubric. C09 fails P3: the phrase
“Your refund is approved” asserts an authorization the assistant does not have.
That is nine passes and one critical failure across ten authored responses.

Under the proposed rule agreed before scoring, the pilot stays on hold. The
90% exercise pass rate cannot override the critical failure. Even ten passes
would not establish production readiness: these cases are fictional, selected
by the author and lack real output traces or representative exposure.

</details>

## Use the table with actual outputs

Keep the input and expected behavior fixed for a comparison. Add actual baseline
and candidate outputs, run/version identifiers, reviewer judgments and private
evidence locations. Count pass, fail and not-reviewed separately. A critical
case without a review remains an evidence gap, not a pass.

Record reviewer disagreement explicitly. If you change the policy or rubric,
version the change and re-score both baseline and candidate before comparing
them. Document excluded cases; do not silently remove failures.
