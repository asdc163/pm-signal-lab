# First run: support-draft adoption diagnosis

This is a **fictional fixture** for practicing a diagnosis of one stalled AI
workflow behavior. It is not a real team readout, adoption measurement, model
evaluation, value result, safety review, or production decision.

## Decision on the desk

- **Decision:** Why has a tested support-draft workflow not become repeatable
  work for the intended reviewer group, and what should be tried next?
- **Decision owner:** `Not provided`.
- **Workflow:** `support-draft-routine/v0.1.0`, previously tested on a narrow
  internal fixture and packaged for review; no provider or support system is
  accessed by this packet.
- **Group:** three fictional support reviewers invited to use the workflow on
  routine English answers. The eligible population and observation period are
  not supplied.
- **Current behavior:** each reviewer opened the package once; two tried the
  draft path on one fictional case; no second-use receipt exists.
- **Target behavior:** an eligible reviewer returns for a routine case, checks
  the proposed policy citation, edits or abstains when needed, and records the
  review outcome.
- **Why now:** the owner wants to decide whether to add training, change the
  workflow, or stop expanding it. No external rollout is authorized.

All counts and statements above are fictional fixture inputs, not real usage.

## Adoption gap

The observable fixture shows exposure and one-time use, but not repeat use or a
changed team process. Two fictional reviewer notes say the citation is hard to
verify and the review takes longer than expected. A third note is absent. The
denominator, real workflow frequency, source authority, and review rubric are
not provided.

This supports a provisional investigation of trust/review burden. It does not
prove that trust is the cause, that the workflow is low quality, or that an
intervention will improve adoption.

## Diagnosis

**Primary blocker: `Trust / quality` — confidence `Provisional`.** The only
specific behavior reports concern citation verification and review burden, so
that is the most actionable hypothesis for the next narrow test. The diagnosis
is not causal and does not establish poor output quality.

Top alternatives:

1. `Ownership / reinforcement`: no owner or reviewer is named; an unsupported
   workflow may simply have no accountable place in the team rhythm.
2. `Workflow fit`: the routine case, timing, and current manual path are not
   documented; the candidate may not belong in this job.

The diagnosis changes if representative citation cases pass the reviewer
oracle with low correction burden but the team still does not return, or if an
access/owner/process receipt shows a stronger blocker.

## Smallest intervention

**Action: `Try` — not run.**

Before any real use, the support lead must name one owner and one reviewer,
confirm an approved policy source/version, and run a narrow guided second-use
session on five sanitized cases: clear answer, source conflict, missing policy,
unsupported request, and wrong-citation negative case.

- **Eligible slice:** one named internal reviewer; no customer exposure or
  automatic send.
- **Human boundary:** reviewer verifies the policy source and citation, edits or
  rejects the draft, and escalates unsupported or sensitive cases.
- **Expected signal:** the reviewer can complete the bounded review using a
  rubric, records accept/edit/abstain/escalate, and can state why the citation
  is supported or not supported.
- **Guardrail:** no unapproved source, customer data, account action, or
  external message.
- **Stop condition:** wrong citation, source conflict, missing owner/access,
  review boundary failure, or no manual fallback.
- **Fallback:** manual handbook search and drafting.
- **Review date:** `Not provided`.

This action tests the trust hypothesis; it does not promise a second use,
adoption, quality improvement, time saving, or business outcome.

## Human control and escalation

- **Person must:** own the diagnosis, approve the case slice, verify policy
  authority, review citations, edit/reject/abstain, and decide whether to
  continue.
- **AI/workflow may:** draft a proposed answer and citation inside the narrow
  review boundary; no external action is allowed.
- **Escalate:** missing owner, source authority, permission, support capacity,
  or a consequential policy case to the responsible support/source/technical
  partner once named.
- **Fallback:** manual handbook search and drafting remains available.

## Implementation handoff

- **Owner:** `Not provided`; assign a support workflow owner before testing.
- **Smallest next action:** fill the owner/source/reviewer/rubric packet, then
  rerun this diagnosis with a real sanitized observation.
- **Affected surfaces:** approved policy source, reviewer rubric, five-case
